# Controlled performance qualification

This design covers controlled CI regression assessment (#261) and the completed multi-process claim-table measurement and locking decision (#266). The dedicated EC2 controller, signed independent calibration and automatic CI path are implemented and exercised with real measurements. The [coverage checklist](../../benchmarks/regressions/ec2-results-2026-10-08.md#ci-coverage-and-timing-decisions) maps that implementation to #261's original criteria. Accepted, regression, invalid and inconclusive remain distinct outcomes; completing the CI implementation does not establish timing acceptance for every revision. The separate upstream SQLite page-cache build flag remains tracked in #348; the maintainer closed #125 pending recurrence.

Analytical reports consume independently signed host and noise evidence through the qualification interface. A matching machine description, clean checkout, repeated candidate runs or a successful correctness test does not replace that evidence. A trusted controller must attest the run's actual exclusive resource control; a separately reviewed calibration must establish noise before the candidate run. The issuer's private key stays outside benchmark code and candidate worktrees. The controller's public verification key is an operator-controlled input, never selected by a candidate report.

Two detached signed documents bind the evidence. The calibration identifies its host/environment, validity window, independently completed calibration, workload identity, exact benchmark inventory, reviewed regression limits, estimator-specific multiplicative noise factors and their confidence levels. The run attestation identifies the actual exclusive-control lease, measurement interval, the calibration digest, exact base/head revisions and executable digests, workload identity and digest of the measured paired observations. Signatures establish the trusted issuer and integrity; the issuer remains responsible for establishing control and noise with independent evidence. Neither document may be synthesized from candidate timings by the verifier.

For a reported head/base ratio $r$, the independently established noise factor $n \ge 1$ bounds the corresponding ratio by $[r/n, rn]$ at the calibration's stated confidence. Given the reviewed regression limit $L$, accept only if $rn \le L$, reject a regression only if $r/n > L$, and otherwise report inconclusive. These cases follow directly from interval containment and preserve the original limit rather than increasing it to absorb noise. Non-finite arithmetic, absent or altered signatures, incomplete inventories, changed limits, mismatched workloads/binaries, expired evidence and overlapping calibration/candidate intervals must not establish acceptance. Confidence is reported per workload; no unproved joint confidence or general engine-wide speedup is claimed.

## Controller contract

The trusted controller owns resource isolation, its public-key configuration and the private signing key. It establishes and reviews the noise calibration independently before running a candidate, using the same workload, environment, sample count, ordering and estimator. A signature authenticates that controller's claim; it is not evidence that an uncontrolled laptop or shared hosted runner became isolated. PR #599 adds the [dedicated EC2 controller and calibration protocol](../../benchmarks/regressions/controlled-runner.md), while the shared-runner analytical workflow stays diagnostic. Deterministic work/memory CI remains enforced. Synthetic signing fixtures exercise only verifier behavior; only actual evidence can qualify a measured revision, and an inconclusive interval must keep its failing exit status.

Both evidence documents are UTF-8 JSON with integer `schema_version: 1` and detached RSA/SHA-256 signatures accepted by `openssl dgst -sha256 -verify`. Signatures cover the original document bytes; the verifier checks them, rejects duplicate JSON keys and non-finite values, and hashes bounded byte snapshots. The operator supplies the trusted public key separately; neither a report nor a candidate worktree may choose it.

The calibration has `kind: "independent_timing_calibration"`, unique `calibration_id`, `host_id`, `environment_id`, explicit UTC `completed_at`, `valid_from` and `valid_until`, and `independent_evidence` containing the reviewed evidence `reference` and `sha256`. It contains the report's `workload_identity_sha256` and exact `regression_protocol`, `estimator: "median_of_paired_slope_ratios"`, and a `benchmarks` object keyed by every regression benchmark. Each benchmark has the unchanged reviewed `max_ratio`, independently established `noise_factor >= 1`, and `confidence_level >= 0.99` and `< 1`. The factor must bound the complete paired-ratio estimator, not an individual sample's standard deviation. The provenance reference must lead to the actual independent calibration and its method; filling these fields with invented values does not establish acceptance.

The run has `kind: "controlled_timing_run"`, distinct `run_id`, matching `host_id` and `environment_id`, explicit UTC `started_at` and `finished_at`, `exclusive_control` containing the controller's `authority`, actual `lease_id` and isolation `mechanism`, and `calibration_sha256` over the exact calibration document bytes. The controller also signs a `resource_evidence` map binding every calibration/comparison resource-record filename to its SHA-256 digest. The per-invocation records contain observed CPU placement, last-observed task runtime/queue delay, before/after CPU and pressure counters, and the measurement executable's verified address-randomization personality and bounded mapping inventory; they remain diagnostics rather than query-latency observations. Its `observations_sha256` is `performance_qualification.digest(performance_qualification.observations(report))`. This canonical digest binds the exact paired samples, workload and sampling protocol, base/head revisions and executable hashes, and calculated regression ratios and limits. Calibration must finish before the measurement and its validity interval must cover the whole measurement. Historical evidence remains verifiable after its validity window; the window restricts measurement time, not the time the retained report is checked.

After the controller runs the counterbalanced workload exactly once and signs its actual observations, invoke `scripts/check-analytical-benchmark.py` with the original eight `--criterion-root` and eight `--baseline-criterion-root` inputs, `--baseline-manifest`, full `--baseline-revision`, both `--head-executable` and `--baseline-executable`, and both `--regression-only --controlled-protocol` and all of `--require-qualified --calibration <path> --calibration-signature <path> --run-attestation <path> --run-signature <path> --issuer-key <operator-key> --output <ignored-report-path>`. This second checker invocation reads the retained samples; it does not run or retry a measurement. CI must obtain evidence and the key from the trusted controller, preserve the checker exit status, and retain the report, samples and signed evidence together as artifacts.

Schema-5 reports retain the existing diagnostic comparisons. With complete verified evidence they add per-workload `qualified_ratio_checks` and authenticated provenance, and return exit 0 for accepted, 1 for regression, 2 for invalid evidence/input, or 3 for inconclusive. Missing or partial evidence with `--require-qualified` is an error. Invalid evidence replaces a previously accepted report at the same output path with `acceptance_status: "invalid"` and `timing_acceptance: false`; regression and inconclusive reports also never claim acceptance. Without qualification arguments the checker retains `acceptance_status: "unqualified"` and diagnostic exit 0. External SQLite/DuckDB ratios remain advisory; qualification applies only to the attested UQA head/base regression inventory, not general cross-engine superiority or independent reproduction.

## Median noise bound

Let the true candidate/reference performance ratio be $R>0$. For each of the 32 reference-only paired estimates define $E_i=\max(a_i/b_i,b_i/a_i)$. Let $n$ be their third-largest value and let $Z_1,\ldots,Z_8$ be the future observed candidate/reference ratios. Their absolute normalized errors are $F_j=\max(Z_j/R,R/Z_j)$. Assume the 40 absolute errors $E_1,\ldots,E_{32},F_1,\ldots,F_8$ are exchangeable within the controlled session; this is the same distributional assumption used by the earlier maximum-based bound, with a different predeclared sample count. Calibration uses no candidate timings.

Write $r=(Z_{(4)}+Z_{(5)})/2$. If at most three future errors exceed $n$, at least five ratios belong to $[R/n,Rn]$, so both middle order statistics and their average belong to that interval. Therefore $r/R$ can leave $[1/n,n]$ only if at least four future errors exceed $n$. Because only two reference errors can be strictly greater than their third-largest error, such a failure requires at least four future errors among the largest six of the combined forty errors. Under exchangeable ranks, the labels in those six positions are a uniform six-element subset of the 40 observations. Consequently,

$$
\Pr(R\notin[r/n,rn])\leq\frac{\binom{8}{4}\binom{32}{2}+\binom{8}{5}\binom{32}{1}+\binom{8}{6}}{\binom{40}{6}}=\frac{87}{9139}<0.01.
$$

Strict exceedance makes ties conservative: random tie-breaking can only enlarge the adverse rank event. This proves marginal coverage of at least $9052/9139$, and an incorrect acceptance of $R>L$ is contained in this same failure event when the interval upper endpoint is at most the unchanged limit $L$. It does not establish simultaneous coverage over all workloads or protect against drift that violates exchangeability.

The prior four-pair rule required at least two future errors above the maximum reference error, giving coverage $104/105$. With eight comparisons, using the third-largest error gives slightly higher coverage and allows the median to tolerate sparse large errors. All 32 observations remain part of the ordered calibration and its signed evidence; a third large reference error increases the bound. This is a change to the predeclared experiment, not a retrospective reclassification of the earlier four-pair runs. Use fresh reference-only calibration and eight new comparison pairs after deployment. The immutable analytical fixture and its four-pair advisory report remain unchanged; `--controlled-protocol` explicitly selects the eight-pair controlled comparison. Historical four-pair signed reports remain verifiable with their original evidence.

The rank test independently enumerates all 64 allocations of future/reference labels to the six highest positions, weights their completions over the remaining 34 ranks, and recovers $87/9139$. Further tests cover ties, both error directions, the third-largest threshold, arithmetic overflow even in larger errors, three versus four adverse future pairs, an unchanged-limit 20% regression, and rejection of four-pair evidence for an eight-pair report.
