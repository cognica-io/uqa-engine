The decision for this workload is to retain the shared mutex and existing bounded batching. At eight processes, the 512-key case completes 4,096 disjoint claims in 1.93 ms and releases them in 2.07 ms; aggregate throughput is approximately 2.2 and 2.4 times the single-process figures. This cost does not justify adding partition-level growth, recovery and mixed-owner admission protocols without an application workload requiring a higher coordination rate or demonstrating that the mutex dominates its latency. The completed #266 decision does not promise linear scaling: throughput plateaus around four processes, and the 64-key case is slower at eight than four. These are descriptive scaling observations, not a separately qualified eight-versus-one speedup claim or whole-SQL transaction throughput. Revisit partitioning if an application profile identifies this observed ceiling as its limiting resource; the present measurements do not require a sidecar-format change.

PRs #599 and #600 provide the controlled infrastructure and correct systemd executable resolution. Their preserved task ledger remains in [controlled-runner.md](controlled-runner.md) and the [qualification design](../../docs/design/performance-qualification.md).
